Understanding Variation Order Claims in Saudi Construction Contracts

Practical legal guidance from a Saudi construction and contracting specialist.

Contracts · Saudi Arabia · Updated 2026

Variation orders are common in construction projects, but an unrecorded change can become a difficult claim. The legal and commercial position usually depends on the contract, the instruction, the work performed, and the evidence preserved by both sides.

1. Identify the contractual mechanism

Start with the clauses governing changes, instructions, valuation, notice, and time. Confirm who can issue an instruction and whether the contract requires written notice within a defined period. A site conversation may explain the change, but it should not replace the contractual record.

2. Build the evidence file early

Keep the instruction, drawings, correspondence, programme impact, labour and material records, photographs, quotations, invoices, and daily reports together. A clear chronology helps distinguish the original scope from the changed work and makes legal risk easier to assess.

3. Separate entitlement from valuation

First establish whether the change is contractually compensable. Then value the work using the agreed rates, reasonable rates, or another method allowed by the contract. Mixing entitlement and valuation too early can obscure the strongest part of the claim.

4. Resolve issues before they become disputes

Early negotiation is often more effective when the parties exchange a short issue list, supporting records, and a realistic commercial position. If the issue remains unresolved, preserve the notice trail and prepare the matter for the contract’s escalation, arbitration, or court route.
